## 9. Validation-only cities (confirmed, encoding caution)

- **8 cities** appear as pickup/delivery in `validation.csv` that never appear in `train_test.csv`: **Allentown, Charlotte, Chicago, Jackson, Knoxville, Laredo, Norfolk, San Diego**.
- This rules out naive city one-hot encoding: a model fit only on train-seen city dummies will have no learned coefficient for these 8 cities at inference time. Any city-based feature needs a fallback (e.g. lat/lon-based features, regional grouping, or a hashing/embedding approach that degrades gracefully for unseen categories) rather than a fixed one-hot vocabulary.

## 10. Summary of key takeaways

- Dataset sizes and date ranges confirmed: 48,000 train rows (2025-01-01 to 2025-10-31), 12,000 validation rows (2025-11-01 to 2025-12-31, genuinely out-of-time), 31 December chart rows (fixed Lexington->Fort Wayne lane).
- `weight` has a sign-flip data quality issue (292 negative in train, 145 in validation) that is fixed with `.abs()`, not row-dropping — confirmed the negative-magnitude distribution matches the positive one.
- `weight` and `market_index` have low missingness (~0.6% and ~0.8-2%); both median-imputed with `*_was_missing` flags retained.
- `distance` is the dominant predictor (corr ~0.91 with `posted_rate`); `equipment` adds a clean $/mile ordering (Dry Van < Flatbed < Reefer).
- `market_index` and `quote_signal` add little beyond distance+equipment (<0.1 correlation with the distance-detrended residual) — they may still help at the margins but are not primary drivers.
- `market_index` has a real seasonal arc across train (low Jan/Sep, peak near May) and validation's Nov-Dec average sits below the train yearly mean — any model or feature relying on `market_index` needs to account for this shift rather than assume train-period stationarity.
- 8 cities appear only in validation, never in train — naive one-hot city encoding will not generalize to them; plan features accordingly.
